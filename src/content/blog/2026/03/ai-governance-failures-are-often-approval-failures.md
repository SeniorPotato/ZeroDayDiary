When an AI deployment goes wrong, attention usually lands on the output or the model. But many of the most consequential governance failures begin much earlier in the process, when systems are approved, integrated, or scaled without clear ownership of risk.

Approval failures can look ordinary: no one owns fallback procedures, product teams assume legal has cleared something it has only glanced at, safety controls are treated as optional blockers, or responsibility is spread across so many teams that no one is truly accountable. By the time the system fails in public, the deeper governance failure has already happened.

That makes approval pathways worth covering as a risk surface in their own right. A durable AI archive should track not only what broke, but how the organisation allowed it to become normal enough to ship.
