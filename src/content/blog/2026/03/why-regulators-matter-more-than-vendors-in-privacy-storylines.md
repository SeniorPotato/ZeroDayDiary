Vendor framing can be useful for understanding what a company wants readers to believe, but privacy storylines become much more meaningful when they are anchored to regulators, courts, and institutional power.

That is because the practical consequences of privacy change often come from enforcement posture, reporting requirements, consent interpretation, procurement rules, and what institutions decide they can get away with. Corporate language alone rarely tells that story clearly.

For a publication like ZeroDayDiary, the job is not just to repeat that a product or policy changed. It is to ask whether a regulator responded, whether the legal position shifted, and whether ordinary people or public institutions now face a different surveillance or data-use environment as a result.
