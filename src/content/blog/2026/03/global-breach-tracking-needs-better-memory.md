Security reporting is often strongest at the moment of shock and weakest during the phase when consequences become visible. Initial breach coverage tends to focus on discovery, attribution claims, and immediate damage estimates. What gets lost is the longer arc: secondary extortion, regulatory response, downstream fraud, procurement consequences, and what the incident revealed about defensive assumptions.

A site like ZeroDayDiary is more useful if it treats incidents as living events rather than isolated headlines. That means tracking when a breach stops being a technical story and becomes a governance story, a liability story, or a public trust story.

The value is not in publishing faster than everyone else. The value is in preserving continuity after the first burst of attention has moved elsewhere.
