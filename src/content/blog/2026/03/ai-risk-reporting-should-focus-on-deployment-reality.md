AI risk reporting often swings between two extremes: marketing-driven optimism and abstract doom. Both can miss the real operational terrain. In practice, many meaningful risk signals show up in deployment decisions, access controls, procurement shortcuts, oversight failures, and incentives that reward scale before safety.

That means a useful AI risk journal should pay attention to what systems are being connected to, who can trigger them, what fallback controls exist, and how failure is handled when things go wrong.

The most revealing questions are usually boring in the best possible way: what was shipped, who approved it, what safeguards were bypassed, and which parts of the organisation absorbed the risk without naming it.
