ZeroDayDiary is now moving from setup mode into content operations mode.

That means the site will publish in three main forms. First, short watchlists will capture fresh signals when several developments are worth logging together. Second, event entries will document discrete incidents or policy shifts when facts are stable enough to support a durable archive record. Third, analysis pieces will connect patterns that only become visible when multiple events are viewed together.

This cadence matters because the goal is not to produce constant volume. The goal is to build an archive that becomes more useful over time. Some days that means a concise monitoring note. Other days it means a single event entry with clear stakes. And sometimes the real value lies in stepping back and showing how privacy, security, and AI governance failures are beginning to overlap.

The rule from here is simple: if a post does not improve the archive, it should not be published.
