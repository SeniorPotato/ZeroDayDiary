# ZeroDayDiary Editorial Style Guide

## Mission
ZeroDayDiary is a one-stop journal for globally relevant security incidents, privacy changes, platform governance developments, and AI risk events. The site exists to capture signal, not to farm outrage or recycle hype.

## Operating constraints
- No email list management for now.
- No direct collection of personal data unless there is a future, explicit operational need.
- No dependence on newsletter funnels or lead-generation patterns.
- The site should remain lightweight, reviewable, and globally accessible.

## Editorial promise
Every post should help a reader answer at least one of these questions:
- What happened?
- Why does it matter operationally or politically?
- Who is affected?
- What changed compared with the previous state?
- What should the reader watch next?

## Tone
- Calm, exact, and unsensational.
- Serious without sounding theatrical.
- Analytical rather than moralising.
- Clear enough for an informed generalist; sharp enough for operators and policy readers.

## Post structure
1. **Headline** — factual, specific, compact.
2. **Standfirst** — one-sentence explanation of why the event matters.
3. **What happened** — concrete event summary.
4. **Why it matters** — operational / regulatory / societal significance.
5. **What to watch next** — next checkpoints, likely developments, unresolved questions.

## What to avoid
- hype-driven language
- vague futurism
- filler background that doesn’t change interpretation
- overclaiming causality
- copying vendor framing without challenge

## Publishing rules
- Draft first when confidence is incomplete.
- Prefer one core event per post.
- Link directly to primary sources where possible.
- If uncertainty is material, state it explicitly.
- Keep claims reviewable and attributable.
